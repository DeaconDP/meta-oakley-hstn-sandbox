SpeakUp is a voice-first assistant for blind and low-vision people, working through Oakley Meta HSTN glasses. This page sets out how it looks, moves and talks. In this identity, legibility is what makes it beautiful.

The speaking lens

A pair of rounded lenses, cut out of a speech bubble. The bubble's right edge is the first ripple, and three sound arcs carry on outward from it. It says three things at once: glasses, voice, and what the glasses see coming back out loud. It is one solid shape with no thin lines, so it holds up at 24 px.

App icon

The yellow mark on Night, lit by a soft violet glow, as if the room is listening. The glow is the only depth effect: no bevels, no gloss. On Android the mark sits inside the 66 dp safe zone, and the themed icon uses the same single shape.

Colour

The palette is dark first: a night sky with one bright signal. Yellow is the brand and the main action. Cyan means the app is listening and Coral means it is speaking. Each state also has a word and a sound, because colour alone never carries meaning.

The voice ribbon

Our signature motif: a soft waveform that shows the conversation. When SpeakUp listens, the ribbon turns Cyan and its waves move in towards the glasses. When it speaks, the ribbon turns Coral and the waves move out. The state is always written next to it in words. If reduce motion is on, the ribbon stays still.

ErrorCoral · stops still · with a spoken reason and an icon

Do and don't

✓ Do

  • Keep the mark solid, in a single colour.
  • Use Night text on Yellow, Cyan and Coral fills.
  • Put a word next to every state colour.
  • Use 20 sp or more for body text and 56 dp or more for touch targets.
  • Use a 4 px yellow focus ring, 3 px away from the control.
  • Give every control a Paper edge, or a fill that reaches 3:1.

✕ Don't

  • Don't put Yellow, Cyan or Coral text on Paper. They measure 1.3 to 2.6:1.
  • Don't use Coral for body text on Dusk. It measures 5.91:1, which is fine for large text only.
  • Don't rely on Dusk Line as the only edge of a button. It measures 1.77:1.
  • Don't stretch, outline, rotate or add gradients to the mark.
  • Don't use the mark below 24 px, or on a busy photo.
  • Don't use thin or condensed weights for text people need to read.

Voice and tone

The app talks, so its voice is the brand. SpeakUp sounds like a calm friend reading over your shoulder. It uses short sentences and plain words, and it puts the important part first. It's warm, but it never gushes. It says what it is doing, and it never says "please wait".

"Got it. Looking for bus 42. I'll tell you when I see it."

Principles

Lead with the answer.
"Gas bill, £42, due Friday." The detail comes after.
One idea per sentence.
The user can't scan back, so short sentences are a kindness.
Say what's happening.
Never leave silence or say "please wait". Say "Reading the page."
